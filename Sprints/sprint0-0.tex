\noindent\textit{Sprint 0 is preparation work. It runs before your first real iteration, so the Common Sprint Requirements document does not apply yet---there is no demo, no sprint tag, and no peer evaluation for Sprint 0-0.}

\section{Purpose}

You cannot build software until you know who you are building it for. Sprint 0-0 is where your team gets together, and where you generate more ideas than you will use.

The goal here is \textbf{breadth, not commitment}. You are not choosing your project yet. You are putting enough options on the table that the one you eventually pick was actually chosen rather than defaulted into.

\section{Detailed Requirements}

\subsection{Team Roster and Contact Information (10 pts)}
Submit your team name, every member's full name, UCCS email, GitHub username, and Discord username. Confirm that every member can be reached through at least two of those channels.

\subsection{Three Distinct Project Ideas (30 pts)}
Propose \textbf{three} candidate SaaS applications. They must be genuinely distinct---three variations on the same idea will not receive credit. For each idea give a working title and two to three sentences describing what the application does.

Remember that you will be building this for four sprints, and that another team will act as your customer. Pick problems that someone other than your team actually has.

\subsection{Problem Statement for Each Idea (30 pts)}
For each of your three ideas, write a short paragraph covering:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the core problem the application solves;
    \item who has that problem, described as a role rather than a person; and
    \item why solving it matters to them.
\end{itemize}

Describe the \textbf{problem}, not the feature list. ``Users can log in and see a dashboard'' is a feature. ``Students lose track of which assignments are actually urgent'' is a problem.

\subsection{Preliminary Feasibility Notes (20 pts)}
For each idea, note briefly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item what the application would need to store;
    \item whether it depends on any external service or API, and whether that service is free;
    \item anything your team does not currently know how to build; and
    \item the single biggest risk to finishing it in four sprints.
\end{itemize}

Honest uncertainty is worth full credit here. Pretending you have no risks is not.

\subsection{Team Working Agreement (10 pts)}
One page. How and where will your team communicate, how often will you meet, and how will you decide something when the team disagrees? What is your expectation for responding to a teammate?

You will be held to this agreement, so write one you can actually keep.
