\section{Purpose}

In Sprint 0-0 you generated options. Now you commit to one, and you justify the commitment.

You will also be paired with another team who acts as your \textbf{customer}, and you will act as the customer for a different team. From this point forward, the application you build is not the application you personally want---it is the application your customer needs. Getting comfortable with that distinction is most of the point of this course.

\section{Detailed Requirements}

\subsection{Project Selection and Justification (20 pts)}
State which of your three Sprint 0-0 ideas you selected. Then explain why, in terms your team can defend:
\begin{itemize}
    \item why this problem is worth solving;
    \item why your team is capable of building it in four sprints; and
    \item what you gave up by not choosing the other two.
\end{itemize}

If you have chosen an idea that was not among your original three, say so and explain what changed.

\subsection{Customer Understanding: Problem and Value (20 pts)}
One paragraph covering:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a clear definition of the core customer need;
    \item why that need matters; and
    \item how your system addresses it.
\end{itemize}

Full credit requires explaining \textbf{value}. A list of features is not a value statement.

\subsection{Customer Understanding: Stakeholders (20 pts)}
A second paragraph identifying:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the types of stakeholders involved, described as roles rather than named individuals;
    \item which of them are your primary users, and how they differ from your other stakeholders; and
    \item who ultimately defines whether this project succeeded.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{In-Class Idea Presentation (20 pts)}
Present your selected project to the class on Tuesday, September 22. Keep it short. Cover the problem, who has it, what you intend to build, and the single thing you are least sure about.

Every team member should speak.

\subsection{Customer Meeting Notes (15 pts)}
Meet with your assigned customer team before the deadline. Submit notes recording:
\begin{itemize}
    \item when you met and who attended;
    \item what your customer said they actually want;
    \item where their expectations differed from your assumptions; and
    \item what you agreed to follow up on.
\end{itemize}

The disagreements are the valuable part. A set of notes in which the customer agreed with everything usually means you presented conclusions instead of asking questions.

\subsection{GitHub Repository Created (5 pts)}
Create your team repository in the course GitHub organization. Add every team member and both instructors. Include a README naming the project and listing the team.
